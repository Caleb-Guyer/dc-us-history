# Short Chapter 9 and 11 campaigns · 7.1.0

[Chapter 9: The Price of Progress](chapter9.html) · [Chapter 11: Across the Divide](chapter11.html) · [Test review](test911.html)

Each campaign takes about 3–5 minutes, plus time spent considering decisions and final recall. Three action missions last at most 36 active seconds each. The clock pauses for two historical decisions per mission. Animated scenes can advance automatically or be skipped; no timer applies to the six final review questions.

| Campaign | Mission | What the action teaches |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Lowell mill, 1824 | Repetitive specialized tasks, mechanization, deskilling and employer control |
| 9 | Erie Canal, 1825 | Inland navigation, reduced freight costs and two-way connections between western farms and eastern markets |
| 9 | Lowell worker petition, 1834 | Labor organizing, wages, the labor theory of value and unequal benefits of industrialization |
| 11 | Corps of Discovery, 1805 | Mapping, scientific observations, Indigenous assistance and the absence of a continuous inland water route |
| 11 | San Jacinto dispatch, 1836 | Distinguishing the Alamo from San Jacinto, Texas independence and the controversy over annexation |
| 11 | Washington newspaper delivery, 1850 | The Mexican Cession, Gold Rush, Free Soil and the unresolved slavery crisis |

Use WASD or the arrow keys to move. Hold Space or E to work at the mill; press Space or E to dash in the other missions. A dash provides a short window of protection, then recharges. The visible movement and Work/Dash buttons support touch. Press P or Esc to pause. Losing a mission allows an immediate retry without losing previous checkpoints. Leaving the page retains completed missions; an unfinished action attempt restarts.

Gold objects mark objectives. Collect from at least two stations or paths to complete a mission; waiting in one place cannot finish it. Red obstacles cost condition. Factory spools require work near their station; merely touching them is insufficient. Other missions collect cargo, records or dispatches by reaching them. The simplified courses and rapid deliveries are arcade representations, not literal reconstructions. There are no rewards for killing people or seizing land.

The historical montage uses a different representative participant in each mission. All dialogue is invented, clearly labeled representative speech. Lewis and Clark, Sacagawea, Houston and Santa Anna are discussed without fabricated quotations. The action does not change historical outcomes or imply one fictional courier caused a real victory. Source notes appear in each campaign menu. [The source guide](TEST911-SOURCES.md) records the chapter coverage and factual corrections used by the full test review.

The game highlights important connections across all nine Chapter 9 and 11 sections, with selected canonical questions at the end. It does not replace the complete 210-question bank or 45 bold-term cards. Game checkpoints and final recall use a separate campaign record. They never grant automatic question or flashcard credit in the review hub. The hub displays 0–3 mission completions for each new campaign.

Sound starts off unless a player previously enabled it. Sound on enables an original Web Audio score, synthesized effects and device speech for cinematic subtitles where supported. Nothing requires a paid service or API key. Voice availability depends on the browser/device; subtitles always remain visible. Quiet QA uses `?muted=1&preview=1`, which starts muted and uses separate campaign records.
