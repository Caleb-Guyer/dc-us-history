## Distinctions to retain

- Early temperance could mean moderation; teetotalism means no alcohol, including beer and wine.
- Moral suasion is a method of persuasion. Immediatism is a demand for immediate action to end slavery. Immediate abolition does not mean all abolitionists endorsed violence.
- Colonization proposed relocating Black Americans rather than establishing equality in the United States. Its racist assumptions are explained rather than adopted as a neutral solution.
- Phrenology’s skull-reading claims are discredited pseudoscience. Its nineteenth-century popularity does not make it a valid modern test.
- Smith founded the Mormon movement; Young led its later migration. Religious beliefs are described as beliefs, not established supernatural events.
- Seneca Falls asserted women’s rights; holding the convention did not immediately secure voting rights or full equality.
- The reading’s 1873 temperance crusades are a later continuation, after the antebellum era. They are not placed in the 1820–1860 timeline.

## Historical checks and source limits

The primary text of the [Declaration of Sentiments, preserved by the National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/wori/learn/historyculture/declaration-of-sentiments.htm), supports the comparison with the Declaration of Independence and the convention’s demands for women’s rights. The [National Park Service’s account of William Lloyd Garrison](https://www.nps.gov/people/william-lloyd-garrison.htm) supports the distinction between his earlier colonization views and his later commitment to immediate abolition and equality.

Two details in the edited reading are not used as date/name traps in practice. Its New England Anti-Slavery Society founding-date line is omitted from date questions; the bank instead uses The Liberator in 1831 and the American Anti-Slavery Society in 1833. Its Fourier paragraph names “Arthur Brisbane”; the period book *Social Destiny of Man* (1840) is attributed to **Albert Brisbane**, as recorded in the [University of Pennsylvania’s catalog entry for the original book](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupid?key=olbp49756). Questions focus on Fourier’s cooperative associations, phalanxes and proposed wages.
