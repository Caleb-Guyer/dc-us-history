# Chapter 5 — historical scope and production notes

**The Last Dispatch** is an original fictional story set against the imperial reforms and colonial resistance of **1763–1774**. It is not a reenactment or a substitute for the whole Chapter 1–5 test. The supplied announcement says five test questions concern key terms; it does not establish the complete test length or say all five come from Chapter 5. The game therefore offers a clearly labeled five-question Chapter 5 drill and an all-15-term review, without claiming to reproduce the test.

## Historical reference

The supplied class list provides the 15 required terms. Additional factual checks use P. Scott Corbett, Volker Janssen, John M. Lund, Todd Pfannestiel, Sylvie Waskiewicz, and Paul Vickery, *U.S. History*, OpenStax:

- [5.1 — Debt and imperial reforms](https://openstax.org/books/us-history/pages/5-1-confronting-the-national-debt-the-aftermath-of-the-french-and-indian-war): Proclamation Line, British debt, Sugar Act enforcement, juryless vice-admiralty courts.
- [5.2 — Stamp Act resistance](https://openstax.org/books/us-history/pages/5-2-the-stamp-act-and-the-sons-and-daughters-of-liberty): direct taxation, the Sons and Daughters of Liberty, boycotts, representation.
- [5.3 — Townshend Acts](https://openstax.org/books/us-history/pages/5-3-the-townshend-acts-and-colonial-protest): indirect duties, Massachusetts Circular, Boston Massacre.
- [5.4 — Tea and punishment](https://openstax.org/books/us-history/pages/5-4-the-destruction-of-the-tea-and-the-coercive-acts): correspondence committees, the Tea Act’s economic effects, the tea ships, the four Coercive Acts and the separate Quebec Act.
- [5.5 — Continental cooperation](https://openstax.org/books/us-history/pages/5-5-disaffection-the-first-continental-congress-and-american-identity): the Suffolk Resolves, Congress, economic resistance, and Loyalists.

## Deliberate distinctions

- Rowan Vale, Thomas Vale, Mara Reed, Isaiah Mercer, and Elias Ward are fictional. Their family conflict, road thieves, armed pursuits, rescue targets, press fire, and escape are invented. The real historical story does not depend on those encounters having happened.
- The rescue mission occurs during the **Boston Massacre, March 5, 1770**. Five people died from the confrontation. The class text describes Crispus Attucks as the first official casualty of the independence struggle; organized war began in 1775. The invented rescue targets are not the five historical victims.
- The **Tea Act did not create a new tea tax**. It gave the East India Company commercial advantages and left the existing Townshend tea duty in place. The chapter includes the tension between cheaper tea and accepting Parliament’s taxing authority.
- The **Coercive Acts** are the four punitive measures. **Intolerable Acts**, in the supplied terminology, includes those measures **plus the Quebec Act**. The game does not collapse these two terms.
- The 1774 Quartering Act expanded troop-housing arrangements; it is not presented as a universal authority to occupy any private home.
- The Tea Party involved the destruction of tea, not a pitched battle against a fleet. Shipboard gameplay is stealth and interaction, with no player gunfire. Some participants used Mohawk disguises historically; this game does not adopt those disguises.
- The Proclamation Line concerned British settlement west of the Appalachians. Native nations retained power and land; British claims on a map did not erase them.
- The **Suffolk Resolves** supported militia preparation and economic resistance. The First Continental Congress did not declare independence in 1774.

## Production

The campaign contains **81 prerecorded synthetic dialogue clips**, about **6 minutes 37 seconds**. Five stock Kokoro-82M voices play the recurring cast. They are not impersonations or historical recordings. The offline model and runtime are described in VOICE-CREDITS.md; no model, API key, microphone or speech service is needed by players. Scripts and clip metadata are in `c5-data.mjs` and `c5-voices.mjs`.

Conversation scenes render in real time with alternate camera shots, character animation, subtitles, pause, line advance and scene skip. The illustrated cover uses original generated artwork; the running game has a stylized procedural 3D treatment. See C5-ART.md. The original soundtrack from Chapter 4 is reused and assigned to each new scene; MUSIC-CREDITS.md lists its compositions and synthesis process.

The game is inspired by the general ideas of a recurring crew, cinematic pacing and an escalating extraction story. It uses no Halo characters, music, artwork, models, dialogue or story assets.
