# The Reform Years · Chapter 13 games

Three short 3D vignettes reinforce the reform connections in the supplied Chapter 13. Each has a 40-second action deadline, two required historical decisions, three opening shots and two ending shots. Gameplay ends when the objectives and decisions are complete. There is no wait for the deadline after successful work.

| Story | Play | Historical connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Shared Bench | Work within reach of three different benches, time fresh taps for precision and sprint to the next job. Held Work remains forgiving. Eight jobs across at least two benches. | Revival, millennialism, Shaker communal property and equality, transcendentalism, and different religious and secular foundations for reform. |
| The Abolition Press | Collect six pamphlet bundles across two paths, vault carts, find gaps and manage sprint energy. | Black abolitionist voices, Garrison and Douglass, moral suasion, immediatism, colonization and political abolitionism. |
| Open the Hall | Set seats, pick up and deliver declaration pages, then hold to place the statement and open the floor as volunteers cross the aisles. | Women’s temperance and abolition work, teetotalism, London in 1840, Seneca Falls in 1848 and the Declaration of Sentiments. |

WASD or arrows moves. Space/E works, vaults or prepares, depending on the story. Shift/C sprints using energy, which recovers when released. In the hall, Space/E away from a task also makes a short protective dash. P/Esc pauses or resumes gameplay. Scene controls pause, advance or skip the film; captions remain visible. Touch controls provide held movement, Sprint and a primary action.

The presentation uses varied sculpted adult faces, fitted period garments, jointed limbs and fingers, and physical props. Joinery, food preparation and bookbinding have distinct work surfaces and tools. Fourteen locally bundled photographed material maps give wood, masonry, cobblestones, plaster and cloth their detail. Directional light and soft shadows ground the actors. The courier’s street recycles past her running character. Fifteen camera tracks settle briefly before moving, then hold their final composition; the depth-aware lens blur is limited to less than three physical pixels to retain crisp edges. Subtitles use compact sans-serif text without shot counters.

The Visuals button switches between Detailed and Balanced. Balanced keeps the materials and characters, using lower shadow resolution and pixel density without the cinematic lens pass. Phones start in Balanced. Scene clocks wait for the material requests to finish; a failed request retains a local procedural fallback. Reduced motion suppresses particles and sprint camera widening. No external graphics or font requests are required. See [art credits](C13-ART.md).

Nell, Elias and Martha and their speech are fictional. The workshop is a composite teaching space; it does not claim Shakers, Brook Farm and Owen occupied the same community. The street route and convention preparation are arcade compressions. Victory does not end slavery or immediately grant women voting rights.
