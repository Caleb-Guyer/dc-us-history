## The test and the rehearsal

The practice bank contains **210 questions across all 14 sections**. A rehearsal uses 14 questions from one chapter and 13 from each of the other two, rotating the chapter with the extra question and covering all sections. This balance is a review design, not a claim about the teacher's chapter allocation. No fixed number of key-term questions was announced for this combined test.

## Historical details kept straight

### Chapter 9

Factory growth changed the organization and control of work; it did not make every worker unskilled or distribute prosperity equally. The review includes workers' responses, women's labor, immigration, class differences and northern industry's dependence on cotton produced by enslaved people.

The First Bank's charter expired in **1811**; the Second Bank was chartered in **1816**. Questions avoid the reading's suggestion that a national Bank of the United States was operating during the War of 1812. See the [FDIC banking timeline](https://www.fdic.gov/history/1800-1849) and [Federal Reserve History's Second Bank account](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/second-bank-of-the-us).

Fulton is credited with commercially successful steamboat service, rather than invention of the steam engine. The Mohawk and Hudson is taught as the early Albany–Schenectady connection, without an inaccurate claim that it was the nation's first steam railroad. See the [National Park Service's steamboat history](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/steamboats-transportation-during-the-cherokee-removal-1837-1839.htm) and [Library of Congress railroad overview](https://www.loc.gov/collections/railroad-maps-1828-to-1900/articles-and-essays/history-of-railroads-and-maps/the-beginnings-of-american-railroads-and-mapping/).

### Chapter 10

Jackson's electoral lead in 1824 was a plurality, not the majority needed to win. The House chose Adams; the alleged corrupt bargain was not a proved secret agreement. Expanded White male voting did not mean universal adult suffrage. The [National Archives' 1824 account](https://prologue.blogs.archives.gov/2020/10/22/the-1824-presidential-election-and-the-corrupt-bargain/) supports these distinctions.

The 1832 bank veto did not instantly close the Bank, and the Specie Circular concerned public-land purchases. The two Cherokee court cases had different holdings, and the major forced Cherokee migration of 1838–1839 occurred under Van Buren. The [Chapter 10 guide](C10-SOURCES.md) links the bank history, Supreme Court opinions and removal records and explains the reading's turnout and campaign-anecdote problems.

### Chapter 11

The Tallmadge proposal restricted further introduction of slavery into Missouri and provided gradual emancipation for children born afterward at age twenty-five; it was not immediate emancipation of everyone. The Missouri Compromise was agreed in 1820, while Maine and Missouri's actual admissions were not simultaneous. See the [U.S. House's Tallmadge account](https://history.house.gov/Blog/2021/July/7-27-Tallmadge/).

The Alamo siege lasted **thirteen days**, ending March 6, 1836. The 1846 border clash occurred on **April 25**, and the U.S. declaration of war on **May 13**. Questions avoid the conflicting duration and dates in the edited reading. See the [Library of Congress on the Alamo](https://www.loc.gov/item/today-in-history/march-06) and [NPS on the disputed Texas border and war](https://www.nps.gov/paal/learn/historyculture/fight_tx_overview.htm).

The Adams–Onís settlement involved U.S. citizens' claims against Spain, not simply a debt Spain owed the U.S. treasury. The later Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo contained property and citizenship-related guarantees for Mexican residents; changing sovereignty did not erase their rights. See the [U.S. House's continental expansion overview](https://history.house.gov/Exhibitions-and-Publications/HAIC/Historical-Essays/Continental-Expansion/Era-of-U-S--Continental-Expansion/) and the [National Archives' treaty text](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/treaty-of-guadalupe-hidalgo).

California's 1858 attempt to bar Chinese immigration was declared unconstitutional and never enforced. The review covers documented discrimination and violence without teaching an effective blanket state ban. A [contemporary diplomatic report](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1868p1/d277) records that distinction.

Free-Soil opposition to slavery's expansion is distinguished from abolition everywhere and from racial equality. “Slave Power” is presented as a political accusation made by historical critics. The Compromise of 1850 ended the slave trade in Washington, D.C., while slavery itself remained legal there. Native, Tejano, Californio, New Mexican and Chinese experiences remain part of the expansion story alongside U.S. territorial gains.

Rehearsal scores and missed-question review help identify what to practice next. The combined test review keeps its own record rather than changing the earlier midterm's completion route.
